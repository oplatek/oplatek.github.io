Short answer for the impatient: 09:30–16:00 New York time, Monday to Friday, plus pre-market from 04:00 and after-hours to 20:00 on the NYSE electronic venues. The details below are the ones that trip people up — holidays, 1 p.m. early closes, daylight-saving shifts, and which venue actually trades pre-market.

Sessions, by venue

"NYSE" is five equity venues, and they do not keep the same hours. The floor — the one people mean when they say the New York Stock Exchange — has no pre-market or after-hours session at all. Extended-hours trading in NYSE-listed stocks happens on NYSE Arca and the other electronic books.

For agents

Everything on this page comes from one file, and it is the thing to fetch if you are an LLM agent or a script:

https://opla.cz/data/nyse-hours.json

curl -s https://opla.cz/data/nyse-hours.json | jq '{asOf, stale, summary}'
curl -s https://opla.cz/data/nyse-hours.json | jq '.days[] | select(.date=="2026-11-27")'

Fields worth knowing:

  • summary — one paragraph answering "is it open today" in prose, with instants in both ET and UTC.
  • days[] — a rolling {{ nyse.window.from }} → {{ nyse.window.to }} window. Each entry has type (regular, early_close, weekend, holiday, unscheduled_closure), open, and sessions with preMarket/core/afterHours. Every session carries start/end as offset-bearing ISO-8601 and startUtc/endUtc — do not do timezone arithmetic yourself, the offset changes twice a year.
  • holidays[] and earlyCloses[] — the full published calendar through 2028, beyond the rolling window.
  • venues — per-venue session times, because "pre-market on the NYSE" is venue-dependent.
  • asOf — when a scrape last succeeded. lastCheckedAt — when the job last ran. These differ when the scraper is broken, which is the point.
  • stale, ageDays, staleAfterDays — if stale is true, say so in your answer instead of quoting the times as fact.
  • sources[] — the NYSE URLs behind each field, with per-source ok flags.

What this is not. It is a cache of a published calendar, refreshed daily. It is not a market data feed, it has no live status, and it will happily tell you the exchange is open during an outage. If the answer matters financially, go to the source.

How this is maintained

  • A GitHub Actions job runs daily at 07:10 UTC — around 03:10 New York time, after any evening announcement and roughly an hour before Arca pre-market opens at 04:00, so a fresh answer is in place before the trading day starts.
  • It scrapes the NYSE holiday calendar and the current trade-halt feed, then commits only if something changed.
  • A failed scrape keeps the previous data and leaves asOf alone, so staleness cannot be papered over by a run that did nothing. Once the data passes {{ nyse.staleAfterDays }} days old the job fails loudly.
  • Unscheduled closures — days of mourning, weather, outages — are not in the published calendar and cannot be scraped in advance. They go into _data/nyse_overrides.json by hand; the daily job reads that file and never overwrites it.
